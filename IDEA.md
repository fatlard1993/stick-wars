# Stick Men Wars: what the game is

Built with chase, from their brief: draw stick men, stones, wood beams, catapult ammo and weapons against the clock (each passing a loose shape check, defaults if nothing is kept); the drawings become the battlefield; send stick men to war, last man standing wins; walls crumble, catapults break, stick men are squishy and bloody, weapons as brutal as they are shaped; play the computer or a friend. Grown since, at their asking, into two ways to play.

## War

- Five timed drawing pages: stick men, stones, beams, rocks, weapons. Many drawings a page; different stick men and weapons earn extra men, more stones a bigger wall.
- Each side: a wall of real stones (physics), one catapult of its beams throwing only its drawn rocks (more drawn mid-battle, 10 a battle), an army at home.
- Orders: charge, hold the wall, go home, crew the catapult, repair it or the wall. The catapult aims once and fires by itself; it can't reach past the enemy wall.
- Blades cut where they pass; limbs fly, the dead fade, the blood stays. Last side standing wins.
- The computer: its own shaky-hand army, a plan each battle (balanced, rush, turtle, siege, waves).

## Gladiators

- Four drawing pages, one drawing each: a warrior, its weapon, armor drawn over it, a shield (both optional).
- The duel fights itself from how things were drawn (legs, arms, weapon, armor coverage, shield size); the player shouts charge, back off or shield up.
- No blow kills: the beaten one yields, medics slap him back together, and the crowd gives thumbs up (spared) or down (drawn and quartered).
- An arena with sand, gates and stands full of the stick men players have drawn.

## Shared

- The hall: winning gladiators, fought again by the computer or picked by a player; a thumbs down strikes one out.
- The armory: every weapon drawn, borrowed by players and the computer, voted on, with kills counted.
- The crowd: every stick man drawn, voted on, seated in the stands.

## Always

- Pictures not words, pencil doodles not emoji, paper and pencil throughout; phones and computers both; two players on their own devices.
